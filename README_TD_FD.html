TD_FD: joint time–frequency tiling branch readme

Noise parameters on tiles in time and frequency, for artefacts that separate from the signal on neither axis alone.

Why the branch exists

A frequency band integrates over all times; a time chunk integrates over all frequencies. Neither can isolate a feature localised in both, and GW191109 is the case that shows it costing something real. Inside the 0.2 s chunk holding the merger, the L1 scattering arch sits at 20–30 Hz while the signal sits at 35–120 Hz. A band wide enough to cover the arch also covers two seconds of inspiral; a chunk covering the arch also covers the merger.

Construction

The residual is whitened once, globally, with the segment PSD,

w(f) = r(f) ⁄ √( Sn(f) T ⁄ 4 )

and inverse-transformed. The result is white, so chunking it in time and taking each chunk's DFT gives coefficients of uniform expected variance, and by Parseval

Σk weightk |Xk|² ⁄ L = Σt w(t)²

exactly, for any set of boundaries. With every tile at its Gaussian limit the likelihood therefore reproduces the Whittle likelihood to machine precision — measured at 2×10−16 in the prototype and 10−10 through the class for tilings up to 9 × 5. Colour is handled once and the tile parameters carry only departures from the segment PSD.

Each bin's q has mean 2 under the null, matching the frequency-domain convention, so the existing hyperbolic and Student-t density code is reused unchanged. Bins 0 and Nyquist carry one degree of freedom rather than two and lie outside any analysis band; they are held at the Gaussian limit.

The rejected alternative. Chunking the raw strain and estimating a short-time spectrum per chunk. A 0.2 s chunk has 5 Hz resolution against a PSD spanning five orders of magnitude, so leakage dominates and the Gaussian limit does not reproduce the standard likelihood. Whitening first is what makes the tiling a reparameterisation rather than an approximation.

Parameters

One set of noise parameters per tile, named from the requested boundaries so a prior file and a likelihood agree without either knowing the sample grid:

delta_1p9_2p1_20_35        # shared across detectors
delta_L1_1p9_2p1_20_35     # with detector_dependent_noise=True

Decimal points become p and minus signs m. Time labels are seconds from the segment start, frequency labels are Hz.

--shared-alpha is the default in the runbooks and matters more here than for plain bands: a 0.2 s × 15 Hz tile holds three bins, and two hyperbolic shape parameters are not identifiable from three bins. Prefer --tile-noise-model gaussian or a free scale in small tiles.

Submitting a run

python submit_runs_real_data.py \
  --event GW191109_010717 \
  --likelihood tiled \
  --tile-noise-model hyperbolic \
  --time-band-boundaries 1.9,2.1 \
  --frequency-band-edges 20,35,448 \
  --shared-alpha \
  --outdir-label arch_tile

This writes an ini with likelihood-type=bilby.gw.likelihood.TimeFrequencyTiledGravitationalWaveTransient and the matching extra-likelihood-kwargs, plus a prior declaring one parameter per tile. Runs are named tiled_<model>_T<n>xF<m> by their tiling.

Never put a boundary on the merger. A boundary that bisects it destroys 20–30% of a deviation's log-likelihood evidence for purely numerical reasons, while a chunk that contains it costs under a nat. Equal-width time_bands=N is unusable at 4 s / 1024 Hz, because the merger sits at sample 2048, which every power-of-two chunk length divides exactly.

Choosing a tile

suggest_likelihood.py scans bands, chunks and tiles and prints the reweighting command for the best candidate. Two statistics drive it.

Λ — is there anything to see?

Λ = n (L⋆ − 1 − ln L⋆), L⋆ = 1 + ϱ² ⁄ 2n ≈ ϱ⁴ ⁄ 8n

Detectability grows as the fourth power of the artefact's SNR and falls linearly with the size of the region the parameter is shared over. Shrinking a region raises Λ — which is the quantitative case for tiling.

κ — a tail, or just the wrong PSD?

κ = ⟨q²⟩ ⁄ ⟨q⟩² = 2 for Gaussian noise, exactly

κ is invariant under q → cq, so it is blind to the PSD normalisation and responds only to shape. Null scatter is 2/√n.

Λκinterpretation
high≈ 2PSD mis-estimated — use a free scale
high> 2genuine heavy tail — Student-t or hyperbolic
low> 2a tail carrying no excess power
low≈ 2nothing an amplitude model can model

Detectability is not relevance. An artefact disjoint from the signal is a constant in the likelihood and biases nothing, however loud. On GW191109 the H1 artefact gives Λ = 11.9 and distorts the likelihood shape by exactly 0.000, while the quiet L1 arch under the merger carries all of the bias. Read the f_sig column before acting on Λ.

Limits

  • Tiles are small by construction, so their parameters are weakly identified. Three bins cannot determine two shape parameters.
  • Chunking still discards cross-chunk covariance. That is harmless for inference — hundreds of nats cancel between hypotheses — provided no boundary bisects the merger.
  • This remains an amplitude model: it downweights, never subtracts. Where an artefact overlaps the signal closely, only a coherent model that fits and removes a waveform will help.
  • No TD_FD container image is registered in container_creation/container_images.json; one must be built before cluster submission.
